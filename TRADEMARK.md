## The license covers the code, not the name

The source code of this project is released under the AGPL-3.0-or-later. That
license grants you broad rights over the **code**: to use it, study it, modify
it and redistribute it, under the conditions the license states.

It grants you **no rights over the trademarks**. Trademark law and copyright law
are separate: the AGPL explicitly does not grant permission to use the trade
names, trademarks or service marks of the licensor (section 7).

## What you may do without asking

- Use the name to refer to this project truthfully: "built on Cairn Foundry",
  "a plugin for Cairn Foundry", "compatible with Cairn Foundry".
- Write about the project, review it, teach it, quote it.
- Redistribute unmodified builds of the official release under its own name.

## What requires a different name

If you fork this project and distribute your fork, you must **rename it**. Your
fork may not be called "Cairn Foundry", nor use a name or a logo confusingly
similar to it, and it may not present itself as the official project. You are
free to say that your fork is *derived from* Cairn Foundry.

This is the ordinary practice of open source projects that carry a trademark,
and it exists so that users can tell where a build comes from.

## What is never permitted

- Using the name or logo in a way that suggests endorsement, affiliation or
  official status that does not exist.
- Using the name or logo as, or as part of, your own product name, company name,
  domain name or social media handle.
- Modifying the logo, or using it as the icon of a different application.

## Asking

Any use outside the above, or any doubt about a specific case, is settled by
asking. Write to Benjamin Bonneton. Permission is normally granted for anything
that does not mislead users about the origin of the software.
